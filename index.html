The number, and what is actually inside it

Fourteen awards out of 545. Two of them were a real sale.

The EU tender archive for Bulgarian public contracts, January 2024 to 22 September 2026: 44,493 award notices, of which 740 for industrial machinery. Of those, 545 state the supplier’s country, and fourteen went to a foreign supplier.

All fourteen were opened and read one by one.

  • Five are Rosatom spare parts for the Kozloduy nuclear plant, where an open tender does not exist in any meaningful sense.
  • Six are spares and service on plant that was already installed: a Pekrun gearbox, ZVVZ fans, Toshiba turbines, a Končar system, a Giesecke+Devrient software update for the Bulgarian National Bank, spares for Sulzer pumps.
  • One is not a Bulgarian award at all: valves delivered to Komotini in Greece for the IGB pipeline. Bulgarian buyer, Greek site.
  • Two are genuinely a foreign manufacturer selling a new machine: a 101,702 EUR precision rotary module to the Technical University of Sofia, won by the Polish entity of GF Machining Solutions under a Bulgarian recovery-plan project, and three scrap grabs worth 211,764 EUR to the Port of Varna, won by a Latvian company.

In almost three years, two foreign manufacturers sold a new machine to a Bulgarian public buyer, for 101,702 and 211,764 EUR. Everything else was spare parts on equipment somebody else had already installed, or Kozloduy. That is not a market that rejects foreign machines. It is a market in which foreign machines arrive through somebody who is registered here.

And before you check it yourself: Bulgaria is the most locally captured of the comparable markets. 97.4 per cent of Bulgarian machinery awards go to a Bulgarian operator. Same query, changing only the country: Italy 95.2, Poland 95.2, Romania 95.6, Hungary 95.0, Germany 90.6. Two points sounds like nothing. It is the difference between a market where the local supplier is your company and one where it is nobody.

Method, so you can reproduce it. Source: TED (Tenders Electronic Daily), European Union, API v3. What the work actually is

Not a market report. The commercial work you would do yourself if you had an office in Sofia.

1

Territory representation

A written mandate with a defined territory, an agreed commission and verifiable targets. Exclusive on request.

Any mandates already in place, with their products and territories, are disclosed before signature, so you can check for yourself that there is no overlap with your own line.

2

Public tenders

Monitoring of the notices relevant to your technology and an assessment of whether bidding is worth it. Then the form is chosen: ACSO as lead of a consortium in which you supply the economic and technical capacity under Art. 63 of Directive 2014/24/EU, or ACSO as a reseller buying and invoicing in its own name.

What is not promised is bidding alone. A consulting company has neither the turnover nor the comparable supply record a Bulgarian notice demands. Anyone telling you otherwise has not read one.

3

Private industrial customers

Steel fabricators, water and energy utilities, food processing, logistics. Visits, technical presentation, negotiation, follow-up. In Bulgarian with them, in your language with you.

Here the barrier is not legal. A Bulgarian fabricator can wire money abroad tomorrow. The barrier is more ordinary: who answers when the machine stops on a Thursday afternoon, how fast the spare part arrives, and who talks to a maintenance manager who does not speak English.

4

Installation, service, spare parts

Coordination of commissioning and after-sales on the ground. This is the part that decides the second sale, and the margin that repeats every year.

ACSO has no workshop of its own and no technicians on the payroll, and does not intend to suggest otherwise. For commissioning and calibration it works with an application engineer; for on-site intervention it identifies and coordinates the local supplier and answers for the result. If you would rather keep service in-house that is fine, but somebody has to pick up the phone in Bulgarian.

Nobody is asked to sign a representation agreement at the first meeting. It starts with a bounded, fixed-fee engagement with an end date, which leaves you holding a result even if you then stop. Two sizes, depending on what you want to risk.

The six months are not arbitrary. They are the minimum duration that makes the engagement eligible under the Italian and Austrian funding lines. See your country page above.

First, if you prefer: the preliminary check

1,500 EUR, two weeks. Only the user mapping, the installed base and the TED extract on your codes. It exists so you can decide with data in hand rather than with a presentation. If you then award the six-month engagement, the 1,500 EUR is credited in full against the fee. Deliberately small, and deliberately outside the funding lines, which have a floor of 10,000 EUR and six months.

Payment

Invoiced at the end of each completed month, 2,500 EUR per month of work performed. No advance. It is a fee against activities defined in the contract. It is not a fixed sum set against future commission, it creates no right to repayment or to an indemnity, and it is not a guaranteed minimum under an agency contract.

At the end you are not tied in

After six months there is no obligation to award a mandate. The file is yours and you can take it to any other representative, or use it to open your own structure.

If it does continue

A representation mandate on commission, within the market range for capital equipment, higher on spare parts and tapering on large orders. Bulgarian law, Commercial Code art. 32-48, implementing Directive 86/653/EEC, which you already know. If you would rather have your own national law or arbitration at home, that is fine.

Two things your accountant will ask

The market entry engagement is a service contract, not an agency contract, so no ENASARCO question arises on it at all. On a later representation mandate the question does arise, and the answer depends on the facts: ENASARCO registration follows an agent established and operating outside Italy only where the activity is genuinely carried out abroad. Your accountant should form his own view on that, and we will give him whatever he needs. Separately, the end-of-contract indemnity under Directive 86/653 only accrues under an agency mandate. A fixed-fee engagement does not create it.

For a reference point. The German interim management market averaged 1,320 EUR per day in the DDIM Blitzumfrage of 23.09.2026. At 2,500 EUR per month this engagement is the equivalent of roughly two man-days a month, which is what it is, and it is priced as a retainer rather than as full-time management. There are cheaper ways to buy export support, and it is worth saying what you give up: the cheaper option works from home, in your language, across several markets at once. Here the person is in Bulgaria, speaks Bulgarian to the Bulgarian customer, and the activities of each two-month block are written into the contract. If you are looking for the lowest price, the lowest price exists and this is not it. What it also does not include: travel outside Bulgaria, trade fair participation, sworn translations, and any commitment on sales volume, which belongs to an agency mandate and not to this.

Who pays for it, by country

The honest version, including the country where the answer is nobody.

Italy

SIMEST, Fondo 394/81, Temporary Manager line, Circular 8/394/2023 in the version of 15.09.2026. It requires the contract to be made with a third party service company and defines that company as a limited company including one under foreign law. Minimum 10,000 EUR, minimum six months, at least 60 per cent of the intervention in the manager’s fees.

It is mainly a subsidised loan the Italian company repays. A grant share of up to 10 per cent is available on any one of ten listed grounds, several of which have nothing to do with geography: an ISO 45001, 14001 or SA8000 type certification, at least 20 per cent export turnover, youth or female ownership, innovative SME status. SIMEST decides the application. ACSO is eligible and signs the declaration you attach. It does not file for you.

Austria

go-international, Internationalisierungsscheck, guideline IO-8.
